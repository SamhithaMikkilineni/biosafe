Know the colour code

Segregate correctly at the point of generation

Biomedical waste must be separated into the correct colour-coded bags or containers. Never mix untreated biomedical waste with general waste.

🧤
CATEGORY 01

Yellow

Yellow non-chlorinated bag

Human or animal anatomical waste, soiled waste, certain discarded medicines, and specified chemical or laboratory waste.

Treatment depends on waste type
CATEGORY 02

Red

Red non-chlorinated bag/container

Contaminated recyclable plastics such as tubing, IV sets, catheters, urine bags and gloves, as specified in the rules.

Authorised treatment & recycling
CATEGORY 03

White (Translucent)

Puncture-proof, leak-proof, tamper-proof container

Waste sharps including needles, blades, scalpels and other contaminated metal sharps.

Sharps-specific treatment
CATEGORY 04

Blue

Blue-marked, suitable rigid container

Contaminated glassware, medicine vials and ampoules (with specified exclusions), and metallic body implants.

Disinfection / sterilisation as specified
Important: This is an educational summary of the Indian Biomedical Waste Management Rules, 2016 and amendments. Actual segregation must follow the latest applicable rules, CPCB/SPCB guidance, facility SOPs and authorised waste-handler instructions. Do not handle biomedical waste for practice.

Chemistry in action

Waste treatment & disinfection

Different waste streams require different validated treatment pathways. Treatment must be performed by trained, authorised personnel using approved systems.

⚗️
🧪

Chemical disinfection

Uses an approved chemical disinfectant to reduce or inactivate microorganisms on specified waste or surfaces. Effectiveness depends on the material, concentration, contact time and validated procedure.

Chemistry: disinfectant action
♨️

Autoclaving

A validated steam sterilisation process using controlled temperature, pressure and exposure time. It is used for applicable waste streams under prescribed procedures.

Science: moist heat
🌡️

Other approved treatment

Depending on the category, rules may specify incineration, plasma pyrolysis, microwaving, hydroclaving, dry-heat treatment, shredding or other approved routes.

Process: category-specific
Safety note: This page is for awareness, not a treatment protocol. It intentionally does not provide chemical mixing ratios, operating settings or do-it-yourself sterilisation steps. Never mix chemicals or attempt to treat biomedical waste yourself. Follow the facility's validated SOP and authorised operator guidance.

Why correct treatment matters

  • Reduces infection risks and protects waste handlers and the public.
  • Prevents untreated waste from entering ordinary waste streams.
  • Supports safe recycling of eligible materials after prescribed treatment.
  • Helps reduce environmental contamination when treatment and emissions controls are properly managed.

Health hazards & safety awareness

Safe biomedical waste management starts with training, correct segregation, suitable protective equipment and authorised collection.

🛡️
🦠
Infection exposure

Improperly managed infectious waste can expose workers and communities to harmful microorganisms.

⚠️
Sharps injuries

Needles and blades can cause injuries. Sharps belong in the designated puncture-proof container.

🌊
Water and soil pollution

Unsafe dumping or untreated discharge can contaminate the surrounding environment.

☣️
Chemical hazards

Some discarded chemicals and medicines require special handling to prevent exposure and pollution.

Everyday safety principles

General awareness for healthcare and support staff

  • Segregate waste immediately where it is generated.
  • Use the correct labelled colour-coded bag or container.
  • Never recap, bend or manually handle used needles; use the facility-approved sharps procedure.
  • Wear the personal protective equipment required by the facility.
  • Do not overfill bags or containers; follow approved closure and storage procedures.
  • Report spills, injuries or collection delays promptly through the facility's safety process.
  • Hand over waste only to authorised collection and treatment services.
